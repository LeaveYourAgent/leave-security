# Leave API contract, v1.11 (2026-09-30)

Built and serving for beta testers — status as of 2026-09-29.

**What exists today**

- **Live:** the marketing site, the Terms and the Privacy Policy at leaveyouragent.com.
- **Configured:** the WorkOS production environment (custom domain auth.leaveyouragent.com verified, client ID metadata documents and dynamic client registration on). Section 10's token settings were checked against it on 2026-09-23.
- **Applied:** the Terraform for the three stages, on a small pre-launch profile: the production project, private network, Cloud SQL with IAM sign-in, HSM-backed KMS keys, Secret Manager, Cloud Armor and the load balancer with certificates for api. and mcp.leaveyouragent.com. The Cloud Run services are deployed.
- **Serving:** the Node backend behind api.leaveyouragent.com and mcp.leaveyouragent.com since 2026-09-29: sign-in, records, the contract pipeline, the brands table, billing (section 9, Stripe webhooks verified on the raw body and processed off the request path) and the MCP server. Push notifications and product email are switched on once their keys are loaded. The iOS app is in development.
- **Design:** every route below is specified here before it serves traffic; `mcp.md` covers Leave inside the AI chats.

v1.13.1 (2026-10-04, the plan is $49 a month; additive). The Base plan's price on the web is $49.00 a month (it was $99.00); the trial, the allowances and the $10.00 pack of 1,000 credits are unchanged. `GET /usage` gains `plan.monthlyPriceCents`, the web plan's price for a month in US cents (4900), so the app reads the price there; the price Apple charges comes from StoreKit. Older answers lack the field.

v1.13.0 (2026-10-04, a Web run that does not have to finish inside one request; additive). **Asking:** `POST /web/run` with the body `{ "async": true }` answers `202` at once with `{ runId, status: "running", remainingToday }`; the run is done afterwards by a queued task. A request made while a run is in progress answers the same `202` with that run's id and counts nothing. Without the body the call waits for the run and answers `200` as before; a request made while a run is in progress then waits for that run. **Reading:** `GET /web/runs/{runId}` answers `{ runId, status, newConnections, reason?, remainingToday }`. `status` is "running", "done" or "failed". `done`: `newConnections` threads were written (0 is "nothing new fits" and counts toward the day's four; `reason` "refused" counts too; `reason` "no_strands" means there was nothing to match and nothing was counted). `failed`: Leave could not finish (`reason` "timeout" after the run's deadline of 150 seconds, or "failed"); it is not counted. `GET /web` gains `run`, the account's latest run as `{ runId, status, newConnections, reason? }`, or null. No push is sent when a run finishes. **One thread per note and signal:** a run writes no second thread for a pair that already has one, so `newConnections` counts only new threads. **A run that cannot finish** answers the older call with `503 web_run_failed`, "Leave couldn’t finish looking just now. Nothing was counted, and it can be run again." **What a run reads:** the notes the phone's grant opens, and the athlete's public record as context for each note's fit; no thread starts from the record. A deal made with athletes of another school is kept only when the note names its brand or its cause. **Talk:** what the person says they want, are looking for or are open to is saved as a note in their own words, and the record changes in the same sentence are proposed as well; the receipt carries a row for each.

v1.12.6 (2026-10-04, dates that can be checked; no field changes). **A signal's date is kept only when it can be checked:** the page states it in a machine-readable way or its own link carries the day (`publishedSource` "page"), or the words the crawler's model read it from are found on the page and state that day (`publishedSource` "model"). Otherwise the signal has no date: `publishedAt` and `publishedSource` are null, and it is listed and matched only while the crawl has found it in the last 30 days. **A newer page of the same programme** replaces the stored signal's link, title, summary and date; the signal keeps its `signalId`. **A thread's evidence** shows the stored signal's date facts only while the signal is still the version the thread was written from; after a newer page replaced it, the evidence shows the thread's own copy (`publishedAt`, `publishedSource` and `standing` as they were, `lastFoundAt` null). **A thread's words:** every date is written with its year, and a date the page gives without a year is left out.

v1.12.5 (2026-10-04, Cancel and credits that come back; additive). **Dropping an upload:** `DELETE /uploads/{uploadId}` answers `204` and removes an upload no contract was made from: its record, the stored file if the upload had finished, and a "file" upload's entry. It is harmless after the upload completed. `409 upload_in_use` ("A contract was made from this upload.") once a contract exists; `404` when there is nothing to drop. **Cancelling a contract:** `POST /contracts/{id}/cancel` answers `200` with `{ cancelled, creditsTaken, creditsReturned }`. While the contract is being read or explained, everything is removed (the file, the contract, its keys and upload records), the work stops at its next stage, a result that arrives late is discarded, and no failure push is sent: `cancelled` true. Once the explanation exists nothing is cancelled: `cancelled` false, `creditsReturned` 0. **Credits:** credits for a contract are still taken when it is created, and are returned in full, once, when it is cancelled, when it is deleted before its explanation exists (`DELETE /contracts/{id}` stays `204`), and when it fails. A contract that failed no longer counts toward the month's contracts and pages; its file and record stay until it is deleted. Every contract in `GET /contracts` and `GET /contracts/{id}` gains `creditsTaken` and `creditsReturned` (numbers), and the `contract_failed` push gains `creditsReturned`. **Uploads a day:** the ten counts uploads that still exist, so a dropped or cancelled one gives its slot back; a ceiling of 40 upload starts a day answers the same `429 upload_cap`. **Exports:** `GET /exports` answers `{ exports: [{ exportId, status, createdAt, expiresAt, deletedBy }] }`, the account's live exports, newest first, nothing of their contents: `status` is "building" or "ready"; `expiresAt` is when the download stops working and `deletedBy` the moment by which the archive is certain to be gone (one day later); both are null while it is being built. `DELETE /export/{id}` answers `204` and removes the archive at once.

v1.12.4 (2026-10-04, a signal's date; additive): every signal in `signals[]` of `GET /web` and of `GET /teammate/athletes/{id}/web`, and every item of `connections[].evidence[]`, gains three fields. `publishedSource`: "page" when the page itself states its date, "model" when the date is the crawler's reading, null when there is no date. `lastFoundAt`: a moment in UTC, the start of the crawl that last found the page; on an evidence item it is null when the signal is no longer stored. `standing`: true for a standing programme's page (a marketplace, a collective, an open application page), false for news and announcements, and false on an evidence item whose signal is no longer stored. `publishedAt` is unchanged: a calendar day stored at midnight UTC, shown as that day and never converted to the reader's zone. An evidence item takes the date facts from the stored signal when the Web is read. **What is listed and matched:** news published in the last 12 months and a standing programme in the last 24; a page with no date only while the crawl has found it in the last 30 days; any page only while found in the last 60 days. A thread already made from a signal that is no longer listed stays, with its evidence and its date. A signal's link is the page's own link, and the same programme is stored once. The AI connection's `get_public_signals` gives each signal `publishedOn`, `standing` and `lastFoundOn` and follows the same rule. Nothing says a programme is open now: neither the page nor the crawl can show it.

v1.12.3 (2026-10-04, one free trial per person; additive): `GET /billing/apple/offer` gains `trial.override` (boolean, always present with `trial`): true exactly when `trial.allowed` is false. The app passes `trial.jws` to StoreKit only when `override` is true (a signed refusal); otherwise it passes no eligibility option, so Apple's own rule (one introductory offer per Apple Account in the subscription group) decides, and the app shows the trial only when the server allows it and StoreKit reports the customer eligible. `trial.jws` stays a string in every answer for earlier builds. A subscription attached to an account on a restore marks that account's trial as used. **After a deletion:** an account that had used its trial leaves a keyed fingerprint of its sign-in address for one year; the address is compared once, when a trial is first asked about for a new account, and a match means no trial (`plan.trialAvailable` false on `GET /usage`, `trial.allowed` false on the offer, no trial at web checkout). New refusal: `503 trial_check_unavailable`, "The free trial can’t be checked just now. Nothing was charged.", on `GET /billing/apple/offer` and `POST /billing/subscribe`, only for an account not yet checked while the key service does not answer; `GET /usage` then answers `200` with `plan.trialAvailable` false. Sign-up never makes the check.

v1.12.2 (2026-10-04, dates in emails; additive): `device.timeZone` (optional string, 1 to 64 characters, an IANA name such as `America/Los_Angeles`) on `POST /devices` and `POST /auth/verify`. The server keeps the latest valid one per account and uses it only to write the dates in emails in the reader's own zone, with the zone named ("October 17, 2026 at 5:34 PM Pacific Time"). A name the server does not know is ignored, never refused; a call without one keeps the one held; with none held, emails say Eastern Time. No answer changes and there is no new error. A guardian's emails use the guardian's own account's zone.

v1.12.1 (2026-10-03, consent withdrawn through Apple, store review R9; additive): in Texas, Utah and Louisiana a parent or guardian can withdraw consent for an app on a child's or teen's Apple Account, and Apple tells the server (`RESCIND_CONSENT`, naming only the install, `appTransactionId`). **The install id:** `device.appTransactionId` (optional string) on `POST /auth/verify` and on `POST /devices`; the app sends it whenever StoreKit gives a verified `AppTransaction`, and leaves it out otherwise; a sign-in is never refused for it. **Closed:** every account with a phone on the named install answers `403 consent_revoked` on every `/v1` route, "A parent or guardian withdrew consent for Leave through Apple. Leave is closed on this account until consent is given again."; the routes the minimum build never refuses (sign-out, refresh, the deletion routes, crash reports) keep working, and so does the restore call. The app treats the code like `account_deleted`: its own screen, with Delete my account and Sign out. Those phones' push tokens are cleared; sessions are not ended, since every call is refused on every phone at once. **Open again:** Apple stops the app from launching until consent is given again and sends no notice when it is, so the app running on that install is the signal. At launch, on `consent_revoked`, the app calls `POST /consent/store` `{ appTransactionId? }` → `204`, with `X-Leave-Confirm` signed by this phone's key (action `consent.store`, target this phone's `deviceId`); an approved phone is not required. The server opens the account only for a phone on the named install: by the id it holds for that phone or, when it holds none, the id in the body. Any other phone gets `403 consent_revoked`; an account that is not closed gets `204`. **Told:** for an athlete aged 13 to 17, the guardian on Leave's own guardian link gets one push (`account_update`, new reason `store_consent_revoked`) and one email with the date; nothing is deleted and billing is unchanged. A notice for an install no phone has named closes nothing; it is kept and a person is alerted. **Apple's Declared Age Range:** `storeAgeRange` `{ lower: number | null, upper: number | null }` (optional; Apple's own bounds in whole years, null for no bound) on `POST /account/profile`. It is used for one check and never stored: a range ending under 13 is the existing `422 age_minimum`; a range ending under the age the date of birth gives is `409 age_mismatch`, "The date of birth doesn’t match the age information from Apple on this phone.", and the sign-up is kept; a range older than the date given changes nothing. Left out when the app cannot read it or the person declines.

v1.12 (2026-10-03, in-app purchase on iPhone; additive; the routes answer `503 apple_unavailable` until Apple's key is configured): the plan and the credit pack can be bought through Apple at the web's prices. `GET /billing/apple/offer?appTransactionId=…` → `{ appAccountToken, trial: { allowed, jws } | null, blocked: null | "stripe_active" | "guardian_billing" | "billing_frozen" | "teammate" }`; `trial` is null exactly when `blocked` is set, and an account gets one trial across Apple and the web. `POST /billing/apple/transactions` `{ signedTransaction }` → the body of `GET /usage`; the server verifies the transaction with Apple's certificates, records it once, and grants the plan or the credits; the app finishes the transaction only after a `2xx`. Errors: `400 transaction_invalid`, `409 apple_purchase_other_account`, `409 stripe_active`, `403 billing_not_holder`, `403 guardian_billing`, `429`, `503 apple_unavailable` (nothing was decided; the app keeps the transaction and sends it again). `POST /webhooks/apple` receives App Store Server Notifications V2 (one URL for production and sandbox), verifies `signedPayload`, records each `notificationUUID` once and answers `{ received: true }`; a notification is a trigger, and the server re-reads the subscription from Apple. `GET /usage` gains `plan.source` (`"stripe"`, `"apple"` or null), `plan.environment` (`"Sandbox"` for a sandbox Apple plan only) and `links.webCheckout` (boolean). `POST /billing/subscribe` answers `409 apple_subscription_active` while an Apple plan is live. Credits bought through Apple are granted once per transaction and never expire. Apple's billing retry with no grace period has no access. Emails: a trial acknowledgment when a trial starts (web or Apple), and for an Apple trial one reminder three days before it ends.

v1.11.15 (2026-10-03, store review T3-07; additive): `DELETE /strands/{strandId}` → `204` deletes one private strand at any time, with an approved phone's token like `DELETE /contracts/{id}`; another account's strand answers `404`, as if it did not exist. One transaction removes the strand (its encrypted text and embedding), its key blobs, and every Web thread matched from it with those threads' key blobs, so `GET /web` and the Teammate's view lose them at once. `POST /talk/undo` now removes matched threads the same way. v1.11.11 (2026-10-02, Leave's own crash reports; additive, installed builds never call it): `POST /crash-reports` `{ v: 1, reports: [1 to 10] }` → `204` for a kept body and for one dropped for its shape; `429` with no body when the hourly cap is spent (200 accepted bodies an hour from a verified copy of Leave, 50 otherwise, counted apart), also from the edge throttle; `413` over 16 KB. No `Authorization` is read, and App Check is verified but never required on this route. Each report has exactly these fields, every one a number, a boolean, a value from a fixed list or a short string held to a fixed pattern: `kind` (`render`, `global`, `press`, `promise`, and since 2026-10-02 `test`: a made-up error sent on purpose from the app's "Send a test report" row, kept like any report and never alerted on), `ended` (boolean), `name` (`Error`, `TypeError`, `RangeError`, `ReferenceError`, `SyntaxError`, `AggregateError`, `ApiError`, `InvariantViolation`, `NonError`, `Other`), `messageId` (optional; `read_property_of_undefined`, `read_property_of_null`, `set_property_of_undefined`, `set_property_of_null`, `convert_undefined_to_object`, `convert_null_to_object`, `not_a_function`, `not_a_constructor`, `not_iterable`, `property_does_not_exist`, `max_call_stack`, `invalid_react_child`, `max_update_depth`, `too_many_rerenders`, `hooks_order`, `json_parse`, `network_request_failed`, `storage_not_ready`), `frames` (0 to 50 pairs `[line, column]` of whole numbers, line 0 to 9,999,999, column 0 to 999,999,999), `phase` (`launch`, `later`), `count` (whole number, 1 to 999), `version` (`^\d{1,3}\.\d{1,3}\.\d{1,3}$`), `build` (`^\d{1,6}$`), `os` (`ios`, `android`), `osVersion` (`^\d{1,3}(\.\d{1,3}){0,2}$`), `model` (`^([A-Za-z]{1,12}\d{1,3},\d{1,3}|simulator|unknown)$`); an unknown key or a value outside its list drops the whole body. A new `messageId` value is added on the server first and only then sent by a build, because the server drops a body with a value it does not know. One log line is kept per report for 30 days with those fields and whether the sender was a verified copy of Leave; nothing about the sender is kept: no address, account, device or install id, and the request log for this route carries no address.

v1.11.10 (2026-10-02, store review wave 1d, C1, approved by Chris): deleting an account after losing the only approved phone. A phone that is not approved (or a device-less session) holds no key and reads nothing; while the account has an approved phone elsewhere, it can ask for the account's deletion, which then waits seven days, is announced, and can be cancelled. **Asking.** `POST /account/deletion-request/code` → `202 {}` sends a fresh code to the account's own email (at most 3 an hour, `429`; nothing else is written). `POST /account/deletion-request` `{ code }` → `202 { request }`, the object the `GET` below returns, with `state: "open"` and `runsAt` exactly seven days after `requestedAt`. The code is checked as on `DELETE /account` `{ code }` (`400 invalid_code`, `400 code_expired`, five wrong tries void it, and the session the check creates is revoked at once). Both answer `409 profile_required` for an unfinished sign-up, `409 device_confirmation` when the caller's own phone is approved (it deletes with its signature), `409 no_approved_device` when the account has no approved phone at all (it deletes with the emailed code on `DELETE /account`, v1.11.9), and `409 deletion_request_blocked` (below). The request also answers, before the code is used, `409 linked_athlete` for a guardian with an athlete aged 13 to 17 and `409 deletion_requested` while a request is open (one open request per account); `DELETE /account` `{ code }` now refuses a linked guardian before the code is used too. Nothing is deleted or changed until `runsAt`. **Reading.** `GET /account/deletion-request` → `{ request: { id, state: "open"|"cancelled"|"ran", requestedAt, runsAt, cancelledAt, cancelledBy: "requester"|"approved_device"|"in_use"|null, requestedFrom: { name, model } | null } | null }`: the account's latest request of the last 30 days, for approved and unapproved phones alike. `requestedFrom` is the asking phone as it registered, and null for a device-less session. **Who is told.** At once, every approved phone of the account gets the push `{ type: "deletion_requested" }` ("A deletion of your Leave account was requested from another phone."), the account's email gets a message with the request date and the date it runs, and for an athlete aged 13 to 17 the guardian's phones get the push too. The push and an email go out again in the 24 hours before it runs. **Cancelling.** `DELETE /account/deletion-request` → `204` (`404` when no request is open). An approved phone signs it: `account.delete_cancel` over the request `id` (section 4). The phone that asked cancels its own request with its token alone; another unapproved phone gets `403 not_requester`. In use means cancelled: any other authenticated call from an approved phone of the account cancels the open request on its own (`cancelledBy: "in_use"`); the `GET` and the `DELETE` here do not, so an approved phone can show the request and cancel it explicitly. Every cancel sends the push `{ type: "deletion_request_cancelled" }` ("A deletion request on your Leave account was cancelled.") to the account's approved phones and an email. Both pushes carry the type and nothing else. After a cancel by an approved phone (`approved_device` or `in_use`) the session that asked is ended, and no new request or code is accepted from an unapproved phone for 7 days (`409 deletion_request_blocked`); a cancel by the phone that asked blocks nothing. **The phone that asked is told why it was signed out.** For 30 days after such a cancel, a token of the ended session, from a phone that is not approved, gets `401` with `error.code = "deletion_request_cancelled"` ("A phone approved for this account cancelled the deletion request, and this phone was signed out.") on every authenticated route, and `POST /auth/refresh` answers the same when the refresh is refused and the bearer belongs to that session (`403 account_deleted` comes first, then this, then the plain `401`). The status stays `401`, so older builds sign out as before. **Running.** On or after `runsAt` the nightly job starts the normal deletion, exactly as `DELETE /account` does (section 5): the account is `deleting`, every route answers `account_deleted`, and the completion email follows. A guardian who has gained an athlete aged 13 to 17 by then is not run (`linked_athlete`). A request goes with its account when the account is deleted any other way. **Scope.** The unapproved scope (section 4) gains exactly these four routes. Additive; installed builds never call them. The plan is left alone during the wait; when the deletion runs, every plan invoice paid at or after the request (a renewal, or the first charge after a trial) is refunded in full before the plan is cancelled, and a cancelled request changes nothing. The request object carries `renewalRefundedAtRun` (true when the account pays a live Stripe plan). The cancelled push goes to everyone who got the request push: the account's approved phones and, for an athlete aged 13 to 17, the guardian's. For an athlete aged 13 to 17 the guardian can read and cancel the request (Chris, 2026-10-02): `GET /teammate/athletes/{athleteId}/deletion-request` → the same `{ request }` object, and `DELETE /athletes/{athleteId}/deletion-request` → `204`, signed `account.delete_cancel` over the request id on the guardian's approved phone, recorded as `cancelledBy: "guardian"`, with the same effects as an approved phone's cancel. Only the guardian link, and only while the athlete is 13 to 17 today: `403 athlete_adult` from 18, `403 guardian_only` for any other Teammate, `403 not_teammate` with no link. The guardian's own use of Leave does not cancel it. After a guardian's cancel the asking phone gets `401 deletion_request_cancelled_guardian`, and the athlete's email says the parent or guardian cancelled it.

Companion to `architecture-and-security.md` (the infrastructure and the encryption design) and `mcp.md` (Leave inside ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and other MCP-capable apps). Defines the routes the app calls, their shapes, and the cryptographic framing shared between the open-source client crypto module (`leave-crypto-ios`) and the Node API. Public by design.

Conventions: base `https://api.leaveyouragent.com/v1`. JSON bodies. `Authorization: Bearer <WorkOS access JWT>` on everything except `/auth/*`. Every metered response carries a `usage` object (section 9). Opaque IDs are ULIDs; no personal data ever appears in a path or query string. Binary values are base64url without padding. Errors: `{ "error": { "code": "string", "message": "plain English for the athlete", "retryAfter": seconds? } }`.

## 1. Cryptographic framing

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Record encryption | AES-256-GCM, 12-byte random nonce, 16-byte tag. Wire format `nonce ‖ ciphertext ‖ tag`. No Tink prefix bytes (the server configures Tink with the RAW output prefix, or uses Node `crypto` directly). |
| AAD for a record | UTF-8 of `leave:v1:<recordType>:<recordId>` where recordType is `strand`, `contract`, `contractField`, `file`, `embedding`. |
| Data key (DEK) | 32 random bytes per record, generated on the device that creates the record. |
| Athlete share | 32 random bytes, Keychain, `kSecAttrAccessibleAfterFirstUnlock`, `kSecAttrSynchronizable = true`, no biometric ACL (so it syncs). |
| Share wrap of a DEK | AES-256-GCM with key `HKDF-SHA256(share, salt = recordId, info = "leave:v1:dek-wrap")`, AAD as above. |
| Server wrap of a DEK | Cloud KMS `encrypt` on the HSM key, AAD as above, done by the API when the record is written. Both wraps are stored; both are needed to read. |
| Account key pair | P-256, derived deterministically by rejection sampling: `candidate = HKDF-SHA256(share, salt = "leave:v1:account-key", info = accountId ‖ ":" ‖ counter)` with counter starting at 0 as ASCII; accept the 32-byte output as the private scalar if it is in [1, n-1], otherwise increment the counter and retry. No modular reduction, so the phone needs no big-integer arithmetic. The test vectors pin this. Stable across the account's devices because the share syncs. Public key registered at `PUT /account/public-key`. Used to wrap DEKs to another account (the Teammate) with HPKE (DHKEM P-256, HKDF-SHA256, AES-256-GCM), info `leave:v1:xwrap:<recordId>`. |
| Device keys | P-256 keys in the Secure Enclave, never exported. The signing key (`publicKey`, `biometryCurrentSet` or `devicePasscode` access control) signs device approvals and destructive-action confirmations (section 4) and is never used for key agreement. The agreement key (`agreementPublicKey`, optional, v1.9) is the HPKE recipient of the share when another device approves this one (info `leave:v1:device-approve:<approvalRequestId>`); it is a separate key because the Secure Enclave will not use one key for both signing and key agreement. A device registered without an agreement key (builds before v1.9) receives the share on its signing key. Neither key wraps record keys. |
| Session sealing | The server publishes an RSA-3072-OAEP-SHA256 public key held in Cloud KMS (HSM). The app generates a 32-byte session key, seals it with that public key, and seals each per-record DEK it wants the server to use with AES-256-GCM under the session key, AAD `leave:v1:grant:<sessionId>:<recordId>`. The server unseals the session key through KMS `asymmetricDecrypt` (one KMS call per grant, cached in instance memory until expiry) and the DEKs in memory. Nothing is persisted. Because the private key lives in KMS, any Cloud Run instance can unseal; no session affinity is needed, and no human principal holds decrypt rights. |
| Grant | `{ sessionId, sealedSessionKey, exp (unix seconds, at most now+900), records: [{ id, type, sealedDek }] }` sent as the request body of `POST /session/keys` and echoed by the app in the `X-Leave-Grant` header (base64url JSON) on each request that needs it. Stateless: the server never has to remember a grant. (MCP unlock grants in section 10 are the one exception: they are held in API memory for their lifetime.) |
| Recovery blob | `version(1 byte = 0x01) ‖ salt(16) ‖ nonce(12) ‖ AES-256-GCM(share) ‖ tag(16)`, key `= Argon2id(recoveryCode, salt, memory 64 MiB, iterations 3, parallelism 1, output 32)`. Recovery code: 28 characters from a 32-symbol Crockford base32 alphabet (140 bits), shown once on device. The server stores the blob only. |
| Test vectors | `leave-crypto-ios/TestVectors/v1.json` is the shared source; the server's `packages/shared/crypto/vectors.test.ts` loads the same file. |

## 2. Auth (WorkOS behind Leave routes, designed screens kept)

`POST /auth/code` `{ email }` → `202 {}` always (no account enumeration). The server calls WorkOS Magic Auth with its API key; the email comes from Leave's own domain via auth.leaveyouragent.com. Rate limits per section 2b of `architecture-and-security.md`.

`POST /auth/verify` `{ email, code, device?: { name, model, publicKey (SE P-256 signing key, SPKI), agreementPublicKey? (SE P-256 agreement key, SPKI, v1.9), attestation? (App Attest) } }` →
`200 { accessToken, refreshToken, accountId, isNewUser, deviceId, deviceApproved, approvalRequestId?, invites: { teammate: bool, athlete: bool } }`
`deviceApproved` is true for the first device on a new account and false for any later device until section 4 completes. Errors: `invalid_code` (400; five wrong attempts invalidate the code), `code_expired` (400, after 10 minutes), `rate_limited` (429 with `retryAfter`); with `device`, also `invalid_device_key` and `invalid_agreement_key` (400, checked before the code is used) and `agreement_key_set` (409, section 4; the WorkOS session opened for that sign-in is ended). A known device that is still waiting for approval gets its open approval request back (the same `approvalRequestId`) rather than a new one (v1.9). `device` may be omitted (v1.3): the token is then device-less, `deviceId` is null, and it may call only the device-less routes listed in section 4; the first device registered on an account that has no approved device and no account public key yet is approved automatically (an account that once had devices and lost them all goes through recovery instead). `GET /account` answers `profile_required` (403) until the profile has been created. No private data can exist on an account until it has an approved device.

`invites` (v1.11) is present on every verify: `teammate` is true when an athlete's Teammate invite for this email is pending (not withdrawn, from an athlete account that is not being deleted), `athlete` is true when a guardian's athlete invite for this email is still `invited`, and both are false otherwise. It carries no name, account id or other detail of who sent the invite; the invite email is the only place the inviter's first name appears. A new account uses it to show the Teammate or athlete path before `POST /account/profile`.

`POST /auth/refresh` `{ refreshToken }` → `200 { accessToken, refreshToken }`. Access tokens 15 min, refresh 30 days, rotated on use. `401` only when the sign-in service refuses the refresh token; when that service cannot be reached or fails, the answer is `503 upstream_unavailable` and the session is still good (store review wave 1d).

`POST /auth/logout` `{ refreshToken }` with `Authorization: Bearer <access token>` → `204`, always (store review R14, 2026-10-01). The server ends the session at WorkOS, which revokes by session id: it reads the session from the access token even when that token has expired (signature, issuer and audience still checked, up to 30 days after expiry), and without a usable access token it learns the session from one refresh-token exchange whose tokens are revoked with it and never returned. The device signed in with that session loses its push registration on the server, so a signed-out phone receives no pushes even if the app's own clean-up fails. A sign-out with a genuine access token is never limited; only the refresh-token fallback (no usable access token) is capped at 30 an hour per address (`429`, store review N10). The access token must carry the API audience, as on every other route.

## 3. Account and Teammate

`POST /account/profile` `{ role: "athlete" | "guardian" | "teammate", firstName, lastName, birthDate: "YYYY-MM-DD", athleteFirstName?, termsVersion }` → `201 { accountId }`. Called once after the first `/auth/verify` with `isNewUser`. `birthDate` is always the date of birth of the person signing up, the account holder: a guardian sends their own date, never the athlete's (the athlete's date arrives with the athlete's own sign-up), and a Teammate sends their own. Only an athlete's date is stored (v1.11.3, store review R8): a guardian's or Teammate's date is checked once for 18 or older and not kept, and `GET /account` and the export carry no `birthDate` for those roles. The server computes age from the full date, on the calendar date at UTC−11 (v1.11.4, section 12); under 13 → `422 age_minimum`, and the pending account and its sign-in identity are deleted. A guardian or Teammate under 18 → `422 guardian_age` / `422 teammate_age`, and an athlete aged 13 to 17 with no guardian's invite for the email → `422 guardian_required`; these keep the pending account so the person can finish another way. A second call → `409 profile_exists`. `termsVersion` records acceptance of the Terms and Privacy Policy with a server timestamp (no device signature; the device may not exist yet).

Role `teammate` (v1.11) is for a person an athlete invited with `POST /teammate/invite` before they had an account. It is accepted only while a pending Teammate invite exists for the account's email (the same one `invites.teammate` reports); otherwise the answer is `422 teammate_invite_required`. `firstName`, `lastName`, `birthDate` and `termsVersion` are required as for any role, and the person must be 18 or older (`422 teammate_age`). Under 13 is `age_minimum` as for every role. After `teammate_invite_required` or `teammate_age` the account stays pending, as after `guardian_age`, so the profile can be sent again. On success the account is `active` with role `teammate`, and every pending invite to that email is linked, so `GET /teammate/athletes` lists the athletes who invited it. A Teammate account has no public-record lookup and gets `403 athlete_only` on the public-record routes. A pending invite to that email is linked in the same way when the account takes the athlete or guardian role.

`GET /account` → `{ accountId, email, role: "athlete" | "guardian" | "teammate", firstName, lastName, birthDate? (athletes only, v1.11.3), teammate?: { accountId, name, status: "empty"|"pending"|"active", publicKey, required: bool, linkId }, guardian?: { accountId, name, paysPlan: bool }, athletes?: [{ inviteId, accountId, firstName, status: "invited"|"active", adult }], termsAccepted: { version, at }, publicKey }`

`teammate.required` and `guardian` (v1.11.4): `guardian` is present while the athlete's Teammate is their parent or guardian. `teammate.required` is computed at each request: true while that is so and the athlete is 13 to 17, false from the 18th birthday. `guardian.paysPlan` is true while the guardian's subscription is the one that gives the athlete their plan.

`athletes` (guardian accounts only) lists each athlete invite: `invited` with `accountId` null until the athlete finishes `POST /account/profile`, then `active` with the athlete's account id. Until then that entry is the only thing the guardian can read about the athlete (v1.11): the required-Teammate link is written when the athlete's profile is created, so before that `GET /teammate/athletes` does not list the athlete and every Teammate read route answers `403 not_teammate`.

`DELETE /athlete/invites/{inviteId}` (v1.11.2) on a guardian account → `204`, device-signed (`athlete.invite.withdraw` over the `inviteId` from `athletes[]`): removes an invite the athlete has not accepted, so the invited email no longer gets `invites.athlete` and a 13 to 17 sign-up with it is refused (`guardian_required`). `404` when the invite is not this guardian's or has been accepted (an accepted athlete's account is deleted with `DELETE /athletes/{accountId}`); `403 guardian_only` for any other role.

`PUT /account/public-key` `{ publicKey }` (account P-256, SPKI) → `204`. Set on first device, unchanged unless the share is rotated. It needs an approved device (`403 device_unapproved` otherwise). Sending the key the account already has is `204` again (idempotent); a different key is `409 public_key_set` (a new key goes through `POST /key/rotate`), and a key that is not P-256 is `400 invalid_public_key`.

`POST /consent` `{ scope: "terms" | "third_party_disclosure", version?, method?, evidence? }` → `204`. `terms` needs no device signature; `third_party_disclosure` is device-signed by the parent or guardian (over their own account id) and refused for any other role (`403 guardian_only`). `version` names the exact consent text the guardian was shown; `evidence` carries the guardian's attestation that they are the parent or legal guardian, and nothing else.

`POST /athlete/invite` `{ email, athleteFirstName }` on a guardian account → `202`, device-signed: action `athlete.invite`, target the base64url (no padding) SHA-256 of the email trimmed and lowercased. It needs the guardian's `third_party_disclosure` consent first (`403 consent_required`), at most 5 a day (`429`). The 13 to 17 path in order: the guardian's profile (their own date), the consent, the invite; the athlete signs in with the invited email (`invites.athlete` is true), and the app lets a 13 to 17 date through to `POST /account/profile` as role `athlete` with the athlete's own date; later `DELETE /athletes/{accountId}` (action `athlete.delete`, the athlete's accountId from `athletes[]`) lets the guardian delete it. The athlete (aged 13 to 17) signs in with the same emailed code, completes `POST /account/profile` as role `athlete`, generates their own share on their first device, and the parent or guardian is linked as the required Teammate on the athlete's account (`teammate.required = true`, cannot be removed by the athlete). The guardian's phone never holds the athlete's strand keys; it receives only contract keys wrapped to the guardian's account public key, like any Teammate.
Invite email (v1.10): the invited address receives one email from `Leave <chris@leaveyouragent.com>` (reply-to the same address) that names the guardian by first name and links to https://leaveyouragent.com, and nothing else. A guardian sends at most 5 athlete invites a UTC day (`429 rate_limited` after that).

`DELETE /athletes/{id}` on a guardian account, device-signed → `202 { completesBy }`. The Privacy Policy promises the parent or guardian can delete an athlete's account; this runs the athlete's revoke and deletion (section 5), notifies the athlete's devices, and unlinks the guardian. The deletion runs like `DELETE /account` below: the subscription is cancelled by the deletion task, not during the request, and the athlete's sign-in identity at WorkOS is deleted. The guardian never receives the athlete's private keys in the process.

`POST /teammate/invite` `{ email }` → `202`. Requires a device-signed confirmation (section 4). An address that already has a Leave account is linked at once and sees the athlete in `GET /teammate/athletes`. An address with no account yet stays `pending` until it signs up, and (v1.10) receives one email from `Leave <chris@leaveyouragent.com>` that names the athlete by first name and links to https://leaveyouragent.com; it carries nothing private. A send that fails never fails the request. Signing in with that address, `POST /auth/verify` reports `invites.teammate: true`, and `POST /account/profile` with role `teammate` opens the account and links it (v1.11).
`GET /teammate/key` → `{ accountId, publicKey }` for wrapping contracts.
`PUT /contracts/{id}/teammate-key` `{ hpkeWrappedDek }` → `204`.
`DELETE /teammate` → `204`, device-signed (`teammate.remove` over `teammate.accountId`, or `teammate.linkId` while the invite is still pending and `accountId` is null, v1.11.2); the server deletes every Teammate wrap. A pending invite withdrawn this way no longer counts for `invites.teammate` or role `teammate`. For an athlete aged 13 to 17 the parent or guardian is the Teammate and cannot be removed (`403 teammate_required`). From the 18th birthday (v1.11.4) they are removed like any Teammate: the wraps are deleted and their read access ends, while the plan they pay for is untouched (section 9).

Teammate change of phone: the Teammate's share syncs through their own iCloud Keychain, so the account key pair is the same on the new phone and existing wraps keep working. Teammate replaced: the athlete's app re-wraps every contract DEK to the new Teammate's public key and calls `PUT /contracts/{id}/teammate-key` for each; the old wraps are gone at `DELETE /teammate`.

### Teammate read side (v1.10)

Read-only routes for an account that is the active Teammate of an athlete (a Teammate link with status `active`, not removed, on an athlete account that is not being deleted). Each one needs an approved device (`403 device_unapproved` otherwise) and answers `403 not_teammate` to any account that is not that athlete's active Teammate. There are no write routes.

`PUT /records/{recordId}/teammate-key` `{ hpkeWrappedDek }` → `204` (v1.10.1): the athlete shares one Web thread with their Teammate the way a contract is shared, HPKE to the Teammate's account key with info `leave:v1:xwrap:<recordId>`, stored under the outer KMS wrap; only the athlete's own thread records, `404` otherwise. Once wrapped, the Teammate's `GET /teammate/athletes/{athleteId}/web` carries that connection's `encrypted` text and `GET /records/keys?ids=` returns its `teammateWrappedDek`.

- `GET /teammate/athletes` → `[{ accountId, firstName, lastName, required }]`: every athlete this account is the active Teammate of, or an empty list; `required` is true when the Teammate is the athlete's parent or guardian.
- `GET /teammate/athletes/{athleteId}/account-summary` → `{ accountId, firstName, role, plan: { status } }`: the athlete's first name, role and plan status (the same values as `plan.status` in `GET /usage`), nothing more.
- `GET /teammate/athletes/{athleteId}/public-record` → `PublicRecord`, the same shape as `GET /account/public-record`, or `404 public_record_none`.
- `GET /teammate/athletes/{athleteId}/contracts` → the same shape as `GET /contracts` (plaintext fields only).
- `GET /teammate/athletes/{athleteId}/contracts/{id}` → the same shape as `GET /contracts/{id}`, `encryptedFields` included. The Teammate opens it with the `teammateWrappedDek` from `GET /records/keys?ids=<uploadId>` (section 6), which exists once the athlete's app has called `PUT /contracts/{id}/teammate-key`.
- `GET /teammate/athletes/{athleteId}/contracts/{id}/original` → `{ url, expiresAt }`: a 15-minute signed read of the original upload exactly as stored (ciphertext, AAD `leave:v1:contract:<uploadId>`), only when a Teammate wrap exists for that upload; otherwise `403 not_shared`. A contract that is not the athlete's is `404 not_found`.
- `GET /teammate/athletes/{athleteId}/web` → `{ signals, connections }`: `signals` as in `GET /web`, and `connections` as in `GET /web` without `strandId`, with no strands list. `encrypted` is present only when the athlete has wrapped that thread's key to the Teammate; otherwise it is left out and only the public evidence comes back.

Honest limit: a Teammate sees Web connections, never the strands behind them, because strand keys are wrapped to the athlete only. A thread's own text is a private record too, and no app build wraps a thread's key to the Teammate yet, so today the Teammate's Web view is the public evidence of each connection (the signal's title, link, kind, market, brand and date) without why it fits. Everything these routes return in plaintext is what the server already holds in plaintext; everything private stays ciphertext that opens only with a key wrapped to the Teammate.

### Public record (v1.6, 2026-09-29; v1.6.2)

Leave looks up what is public about an athlete after they sign up, never before, and keeps no profile of anyone who has not signed up. When `POST /account/profile` creates an athlete account aged 18 or over, the API enqueues one lookup for the worker (`POST /tasks/public-record/lookup { accountId }` on Cloud Tasks with OIDC, named per account so it runs once). The worker asks Gemini on Vertex AI, grounded with Google Search, for the public record of the name on the account; an answer below 0.6 confidence, or no answer, writes nothing. The looked-up record is unconfirmed until the athlete confirms or corrects it in the app, and the lookup never writes over a record the athlete has edited or confirmed. For athletes aged 13 to 17 there is no lookup: Leave does not search the web for a minor by name, so their record starts empty and is written from the app.

Current as of today (v1.6.2). The lookup prompt carries today's date, and the record describes the athlete as of that date: the model searches for the most recent news first (transfers, signings, trades and retirements in the last 12 months), reports the current team or school, and prefers the newest dated source when pages disagree. The answer comes from the search results only: an answer made with no Google Search query at all (the model's memory) is discarded and writes nothing, the same as no answer. Stats describe the most recent completed or in-progress season, and the season is kept as a suffix on `stats`, e.g. `24.1 pts · 7.9 reb (2026-27)`. Each lookup is metered with its search count, including a discarded one.

`POST /account/public-record/lookup` (no body, or `{}`) → `202 {}` (v1.6.2): runs the lookup again, e.g. after a trade or transfer. The worker replaces a record only while it is still as the lookup found it (`source` `"lookup"`) and unconfirmed; a record the athlete has edited or confirmed stays as it is, and a re-run that finds nothing leaves the earlier record in place. The result arrives asynchronously, so the app reads it with `GET /account/public-record`. At most 3 re-runs per account per UTC day: the fourth answers `429` with `error.code = "lookup_cap"` and `retryAfter: 3600`. Athlete accounts only (`403 athlete_only` for a guardian or teammate account, `403 profile_required` without a profile), and athletes aged 18 or over only: an athlete aged 13 to 17 gets `403 minor_no_lookup`, the same rule as at sign-up.

`GET /account/public-record` → `200 PublicRecord`, or `404` with `error.code = "public_record_none"` when no record exists yet.

`PUT /account/public-record` `{ displayName?, sport?, position?, team?, school?, classYear?, market?, height?, stats?, followers?, confirm: boolean }` → `200 PublicRecord`. An omitted field is unchanged and `null` clears one (except `displayName`). Strings are at most 120 characters, `height` at most 20 and `stats` at most 200; `market` is a lowercase slug such as `philadelphia`, the form the crawler uses; `followers` keys are `instagram`, `tiktok`, `x`, `youtube`, `threads` or `facebook`, with non-negative integer counts. With no record yet, the PUT creates one starting from the account's name. A field whose value differs from the stored one sets `source` to `"athlete"`; `confirm: true` sets `confirmedAt` to now and `confirm: false` leaves it as it was. No device-signed confirmation (nothing is destroyed). Athlete accounts only: a guardian or teammate account gets `403 athlete_only`, and an account without a profile gets `403 profile_required`.

`PublicRecord` = `{ displayName, sport, position, team, school, classYear, market, height, stats, followers: { [platform]: count }, publicReach, dealsListed?, source: "lookup" | "athlete", sources: [{ url, title }], updatedAt, confirmedAt }`. `team` is the professional or club team and `school` the current school (v1.6.2): a professional has a `team` and a null `school`, and a student who plays for a school has a `school` and a null `team`. `publicReach` is the sum of `followers`, derived on every response and never stored. `dealsListed` is the number of public deals the lookup found: present only when the lookup reported it, and not settable through PUT. `sources` lists the pages the lookup used (empty for a record the athlete wrote). `source` is `"lookup"` while the record is as the lookup found it and `"athlete"` once the athlete has changed a field; `confirmedAt` is null until the record is confirmed by the athlete, which the app shows as CONFIRMED BY YOU. The MCP tool `get_profile` returns the same fields except `sources` and `updatedAt`, with `source` and `confirmedAt` null when there is no record.


For a guardian, `GET /account` also returns `pendingAthlete: { firstName } | null`: the athlete's first name the guardian gave at `POST /account/profile`, so it shows on every device before any invite exists (v1.11.1).
## 4. Devices and approvals

`POST /devices` `{ name, model, publicKey, agreementPublicKey?, attestation? }` → `{ deviceId, approved: false, approvalRequestId }`, or `{ deviceId, approved: true }` for the first device on an account with no approved device and no account key (also done implicitly by `/auth/verify`). Device descriptor (v1.9): `publicKey` is the signing key and `agreementPublicKey` the optional agreement key (section 1), each base64url (no padding) of the SPKI DER of a P-256 key, at most 400 characters; otherwise `400 invalid_device_key` or `400 invalid_agreement_key`.
`GET /devices` → `[{ deviceId, name, model, createdAt, lastSeenAt, approved, isCurrent }]`.
`POST /devices/{id}/approve` `{ approvalRequestId, wrappedShare, signature }` → `204`. An existing approved device wraps the share with HPKE (info `leave:v1:device-approve:<approvalRequestId>`) to the new device's agreement key when it has one, otherwise to its signing key, and signs with its own signing key. Signed bytes (v1.9), UTF-8 of the id followed by SPKI DER: `approvalRequestId ‖ newDevicePublicKey ‖ newDeviceAgreementPublicKey` when the new device has an agreement key, so the approving phone vouches for the key the share is wrapped to; `approvalRequestId ‖ newDevicePublicKey` when it has none. The server picks the form from what the new device registered, never from the request, verifies the signature against the approving device, and stores the wrapped share for one-time pickup. Answers: `403 device_unapproved` when the caller is not an approved device; `404 not_found` when the request is unknown, expired, closed or already approved, or the device was removed; `409 already_approved` when the new device is already approved (for example it activated with the recovery code), which also closes the request; `403 signature_invalid` when the signature does not verify.
`GET /devices/pending` → `[{ approvalRequestId, deviceId, name, model, requestedAt, publicKey, agreementPublicKey, expiresAt, wrappedShare? }]`. `publicKey` (the new device's signing key) and `agreementPublicKey` (its agreement key, or null) are base64url SPKI, the bytes the approving phone signs over and wraps to; `expiresAt` (ISO 8601) is when the request lapses (v1.9). Visibility (v1.9): an approved device sees every open request on the account; an unapproved device sees only its own device's request, never another device's, and a device-less token sees an empty list. The new device polls until its entry comes back with `wrappedShare`, on its first poll after the approval; the share is then cleared and the entry is no longer listed. Or the recovery code path is used instead. An approval request expires 24 hours after it is opened; a waiting device that signs in after that gets a new one.
`DELETE /devices/{id}` → `204`, device-signed.
`PUT /devices/{id}/push-token` `{ token }` → `204` (v1.7): the app's Firebase Cloud Messaging registration, as the Firebase SDK hands it out (the registration token, or the Firebase Installation ID once the SDK moves to those; printable ASCII, at most 4,096 characters). Only the device itself may set it (`403 wrong_device` for another id or a device-less token) and it must not be removed; it need not be approved, so a phone waiting for approval registers first and receives pushes from the moment it is approved. The app sends it again whenever the SDK reports a new one. A registration another device row holds (on any account) is taken off that row, so one phone never receives two accounts' pushes. `DELETE /devices/{id}/push-token` → `204` at sign-out (idempotent); `DELETE /devices/{id}` clears it too. Neither needs a device-signed confirmation.

Unapproved or device-less scope: an access token from `/auth/verify` with `deviceApproved: false` (or no device) may call only `GET /account`, `POST /account/profile`, `POST /consent`, `GET /devices`, `POST /devices`, `GET /devices/pending`, `GET /recovery`, `POST /devices/{id}/activate`, `PUT` and `DELETE /devices/{id}/push-token` (v1.7), `POST /auth/refresh`, `POST /auth/logout`, `DELETE /account/pending` (v1.11.8), `POST /account/deletion-request/code` and `POST`, `GET` and `DELETE /account/deletion-request` (v1.11.10), `GET /usage`, `POST /events`, and `DELETE /account` while the account has no approved device (see section 5). Every other route returns `403` with `error.code = "device_unapproved"`, so the app renders the approval screen from that code. After the recovery-code path, the device proves possession of the share with `POST /devices/{id}/activate` `{ proof }` where proof is a signature by the account key pair over `deviceId ‖ accountId`; the server verifies against the registered account public key and marks the device approved. Activation works whenever the account key is set, whether or not an approval request is open (`409 no_account_key` while the account has none, `403 proof_invalid` when the proof does not verify, `403 wrong_device` for another device's id or a device-less token). It closes the device's open approval requests: they leave `GET /devices/pending` and approving one answers `404`. An already-approved device calling it gets `204` and nothing changes; its approval time stays as it was (v1.9).

Destructive-action confirmation: the app signs `action ‖ targetId ‖ timestamp` with the SE key (biometric prompt) and sends `X-Leave-Confirm: <base64url JSON { deviceId, timestamp, signature }>`. Required on: teammate invite/remove, key rotate, key revoke, export, delete account, device removal, turning on private data for an MCP client, guardian consent changes. Timestamp within 5 minutes.

A public key the account already has (not removed) resolves to that device on both `POST /auth/verify` (with `device`) and `POST /devices`: the same `deviceId` and its approval come back, an open approval request is re-used, and no second device is created, so a phone that lost its session but kept its Secure Enclave key never waits for approval from itself (v1.6). The device is matched on its signing key. An agreement key sent for a known device is stored when the device has none; when the device already has a different one, the stored key stays and the answer is `409 agreement_key_set`, because a device's agreement key changes only by removing the device and adding it again. The open request is re-used on both routes, and `device_approval_request` is sent only when a new request is opened, not on every sign-in; `new_sign_in` is still sent for each sign-in (v1.9).

Device and key error codes in use: `device_unapproved` (403), `invalid_device_key` (400), `invalid_agreement_key` (400), `agreement_key_set` (409), `wrong_device` (403), `signature_invalid` (403), `already_approved` (409), `no_account_key` (409), `proof_invalid` (403), `public_key_set` (409, section 3), `invalid_public_key` (400, section 3), `confirmation_required` (403: the `X-Leave-Confirm` header is missing), `confirmation_invalid` (403: it does not parse, names no approved device of the account, or its signature does not verify), `confirmation_expired` (403: its timestamp is more than 5 minutes from the server's clock), and `account_deleting` (403: the account is being deleted and the route would change it).

## 5. Recovery and key lifecycle

`PUT /recovery` `{ blob }` → `204` (replace). `GET /recovery` → `{ blob }` (only after `/auth/verify`; rate limited 5 per hour per account; each fetch notifies all devices).
`POST /key/rotate` `{ newPublicKey, rewrapped: [{ recordId, shareWrappedDek }] , newRecoveryBlob }` device-signed → `204`. Paged if over 1,000 records: `POST /key/rotate/begin` → `{ rotationId }`, `PUT /key/rotate/{rotationId}/records` in batches, `POST /key/rotate/{rotationId}/commit`.
Pending wraps (v1.10): a record the server created is wrapped to the current account key until the app re-wraps it with the share (section 6), and a rotation replaces that key. So while the account has any pending record key, `POST /key/rotate`, `POST /key/rotate/begin` and `POST /key/rotate/{rotationId}/commit` answer `409 pending_keys` and change nothing. The app drains them first (`GET /records/keys?pending=true`, unwrap with the account key, `PUT /records/keys` with share wraps) and then rotates. The commit checks again, because the server may create a record while a paged rotation is open; a record being created and a rotation being applied wait for each other on the account, so a pending wrap is either seen by the check or made to the new key.
`POST /key/revoke` device-signed → `{ undoUntil }`. The server marks the account revoked; reads fail immediately; at `undoUntil` (24 h) the server deletes every share-wrapped DEK and the recovery blob. `POST /key/revoke/undo` within the window restores. The app deletes the share from Keychain and iCloud Keychain at revoke and can only undo if the athlete kept the recovery code.
`GET /key` → `{ createdAt, rotatedAt, devices: n, recoverySet: bool, revoked?: { undoUntil } }`.

`DELETE /account` device-signed → `202 { completesBy }`. While the account has no approved device (nothing encrypted exists yet), the confirmation header is not required (v1.3). (30 days: revoke runs first, then ciphertext, metadata and backups are gone by `completesBy`). A guardian account with a linked athlete aged 13 to 17 is refused with `error.code = "linked_athlete"` until the athlete's account is deleted or a replacement parent or guardian has accepted, so deleting a parent's account can never destroy or orphan the athlete's data. Once every linked athlete is 18 or older (v1.11.4) the guardian may delete their account; that cancels their subscription, which ends any athlete's coverage by it.

How the deletion runs (2026-10-01): the request never calls Stripe, so it cannot fail on Stripe; the subscription is cancelled by the deletion task, which is retried until it succeeds. The sign-in identity at WorkOS is deleted too, with its sessions revoked first. A finished account gets one email, "Your Leave account has been deleted", when the deletion is done; payment records that Stripe keeps as the payment processor stay with Stripe. A pending account (profile not finished) is deleted at once, with the access token alone (it holds no key and no data yet), and `completesBy` is now; it gets no email.

`POST /export` `{ dek?: { shareWrappedDek } }` device-signed (`account.export`, target `new`), 2 per day → `202 { exportId }`. `GET /export/{id}` → `{ status: "building"|"ready"|"expired", url?, urlExpiresAt?, expiresAt?, sizeBytes? }`.

Export archive (v1.8). The worker builds one zip at `athletes/<accountId>/exports/<exportId>.zip` in the files bucket (usually within minutes; the promise stays "within a day") and then sends `export_ready` (section 11). The server does not decrypt any private record to build it and uses no grant: each private record goes into the archive as the ciphertext it is stored as, so it opens only with the athlete's share. Layout:
- `README.txt`: how the archive is laid out, in plain words.
- `account.json`: what the server holds in plaintext, as it holds it: the profile, consents, devices (without push registrations or session ids), Teammate and guardian links, the public record, plan status and auto-refill, a usage summary per UTC month and feature (events, units, credits), the credits ledger, MCP clients, and the plaintext metadata of contracts, files and Web connections.
- `records/<type>/<recordId>.bin`: each encrypted record byte for byte as stored (`nonce ‖ ciphertext ‖ tag`): contract originals and Markdown (`contract`), extracted fields (`contractField`), files (`file`), strands, including notes added from Talk or an AI client (`strand`), Web threads (`thread`), and the encrypted embeddings of strands and contracts (`embedding`). Talk receipts and pitch drafts are not stored on the server (the phone holds them), so they are not in the archive.
- `records/index.json`: `{ records: [{ recordId, type, aad, keyRecordId, byteSize, object, contractId?, part?, contentType? }] }`. `aad` is the exact string the record was sealed with; `keyRecordId` is the `keys.json` entry whose DEK opens it (a contract's Markdown, fields and embedding use the contract's key, keyed by the original's `uploadId`); `part` is `original`, `markdown`, `fields` or `embedding`.
- `keys.json`: `{ keys: [{ recordId, type, shareWrappedDek?, accountWrappedDek? }] }`: the account's own key blobs with the outer KMS layer removed, the same step `GET /records/keys` takes (section 6). `shareWrappedDek` opens with the share (section 1); `accountWrappedDek` (a record the server created that the phone has not re-wrapped yet) opens with the account key derived from the share (HPKE, info `leave:v1:pending:<recordId>`). Teammate wraps are left out.

The zip as a whole is not encrypted: its plaintext parts are what the server already holds in plaintext, and Cloud Storage encrypts the object at rest under the CMEK key. `dek` is optional since v1.8 and unused, because the server never holds that DEK in plaintext and so cannot encrypt under it; an older app that sends it still gets `202`. `url` is a signed read valid for 15 minutes, minted on each GET (never past `expiresAt`); `expiresAt` is when the archive is deleted, 7 days after it was built, after which the answer is `{ status: "expired" }`. The build is idempotent on Cloud Tasks retries: it never builds or pushes twice. A revoked share (above) makes the records in any export unreadable too.

Export email (v1.10): together with `export_ready`, the account's address receives one email from `Leave <chris@leaveyouragent.com>` saying the export is ready and is deleted 7 days after it was built. It carries no link to the archive and nothing from it; the download link is only in the app (`GET /export/{id}`).

## 6. Session grants and record keys

`POST /session` → `{ sessionId, serverPublicKey, keyVersion, exp }`: `serverPublicKey` is base64url (no padding) of the RSA-3072 SPKI DER (not PEM); `keyVersion` is the KMS key version name's last segment and changes only when the deployed key version does; `exp` = now + 900 s. The grant carries no `keyVersion`: a grant sealed to a retired key fails to open and answers `400 grant_invalid`. `POST /session/keys` answers `204`, `403 grant_expired` (`exp` in the past), `400 grant_ttl_too_long` (`exp` more than 900 s ahead; MCP grants may go to 3,600 s), `403 key_revoked`, or `400 invalid_request`. Routes that need a grant answer `403 grant_required` without the header, `400 grant_invalid` when it does not parse or open, and `403 grant_missing_record` when the record's DEK is not in it. `X-Leave-Grant` is base64url of the UTF-8 JSON of the same grant object (sessionId, sealedSessionKey, exp, records); the session key seal is RSA-OAEP with SHA-256 for both the hash and MGF1 and an empty label over the 32-byte session key; each `sealedDek` is AES-256-GCM `nonce ‖ ciphertext ‖ tag` under the session key with AAD `leave:v1:grant:<sessionId>:<recordId>` (v1.6 wording, matching the code).
`POST /session/keys` body = grant (section 1) → `204`. The server validates the seal and caches the DEKs in memory until `exp`.
`DELETE /session` → `204` (drops the cache early).
Any request that reads private data also carries `X-Leave-Grant`; if the instance has not seen it, it unseals it (one KMS call) and proceeds.

`GET /records/keys?ids=a,b,c` → `[{ recordId, type, shareWrappedDek, serverWrappedDek, teammateWrappedDek? }]` (max 200 per call).
`PUT /records/keys` `[{ recordId, shareWrappedDek }]` → `204` (used by rotation and by re-wraps after a new record is created off-device, e.g. via MCP: the server creates the DEK, server-wraps it, and stores the share-wrap as pending; the next app session fetches pending records with `GET /records/keys?pending=true` and completes the share wrap).

Talk strands (v1.10): when a Talk turn saves a strand, the server also computes its embedding at that moment, from the plaintext it already holds to create the record, as it does for an MCP note: Vertex `gemini-embedding-001` at 768 dimensions, stored encrypted under the strand's DEK with AAD `leave:v1:embedding:<strandId>`, and metered as one `embedding` usage event. If embedding fails, the strand is saved without one and the turn goes on; a Web run leaves out a strand without an embedding.

## 7. Contracts, files, uploads

The phone extracts a contract's text first (PDFKit for PDFs, on-device DOCX conversion, Apple's Vision OCR for photos) and uploads Markdown alongside the encrypted original. Document AI is used only for scans the phone cannot read, and only after the athlete is told and agrees. The worker then runs Gemini 3.8 Flash structured extraction.

`POST /uploads` `{ kind: "contract"|"file", contentType, sizeBytes (1 to 26,214,400), sha256? (lowercase hex of the ciphertext) }` → `{ uploadId, signedUrl, expiresAt, remainingToday }`; `429 upload_cap` after 10 uploads in a UTC day, `403 storage_full` when the upload would pass the 1 GB quota, for a contract original as for a file (wave 1d; before, only a file was refused). `sizeBytes` is the ciphertext size as uploaded (plaintext + 28: the 12-byte nonce and 16-byte tag). `sha256` is optional since v1.6.1: the ciphertext's AAD includes the `uploadId`, which only exists once this response arrives, so the hash normally comes with `POST /uploads/{id}/complete`; when it is sent here it has the same shape and is recorded as sent. The signed PUT (valid 15 minutes) is a V4 signature binding only `Content-Type: application/octet-stream` and the host, whatever `contentType` was declared, so the client sends exactly that header, no `Content-Encoding`, and nothing else is required. The client encrypts before the PUT (record type `file` or `contract`, AAD `leave:v1:<kind>:<uploadId>`); the object is stored and later served exactly as uploaded.
`POST /uploads/{id}/complete` `{ sha256 (lowercase hex of the ciphertext), sizeBytes (1 to 26,214,400) }` → `204` (v1.6.1), sent once the signed PUT has succeeded. The server reads the object's size from the Cloud Storage metadata and records `sha256` as declared; the API does not read the object back. v1.8: the first completion also enqueues a worker check (`upload.verify`) that streams the stored ciphertext and recomputes its SHA-256. A match marks the upload verified; a mismatch marks it altered, and a contract on it that is still `processing` fails with `failure.code` `hash_mismatch` and the `contract_failed` push. Only ciphertext is hashed; nothing is decrypted. On success the upload's `sizeBytes` becomes the stored object's real size, which is also what the 1 GB quota counts. Answers `404 not_found` when the upload is not the caller's, `409 upload_missing` when no object is stored for it, `400 upload_size_mismatch` (and, for a `file` upload whose verified size is larger than declared, `403 storage_full` when the verified size would exceed the 1 GB quota, since the signed PUT does not bound the object's size) when the stored size differs from `sizeBytes`, and `409 upload_already_completed` when the upload was already completed with a different `sha256` or `sizeBytes`; the same body sent again answers `204`.
`POST /contracts` `{ uploadId, markdownUploadId?, ocrByGoogle: false|true, pageCount, dek: { shareWrappedDek } }` → `{ contractId, status: "processing", etaSeconds (45), usage }`. `ocrByGoogle` may be true only after the athlete agreed on screen; the server answers `400 ocr_consent_required` for a scan without Markdown unless it is true. Requires a grant whose `records` include `id = uploadId` (`403 grant_missing_record` otherwise); the worker decrypts with that DEK, so it must be the DEK inside `shareWrappedDek`. `shareWrappedDek` is AES-256-GCM `nonce ‖ ciphertext ‖ tag` under HKDF-SHA256(ikm = share, salt = UTF-8 of `uploadId`, info = `leave:v1:dek-wrap`, 32 bytes) with AAD `leave:v1:contract:<uploadId>`; the server never opens it and stores it under an outer KMS wrap. The optional Markdown upload is encrypted with the same contract DEK and AAD `leave:v1:contract:<markdownUploadId>`; its id need not be in the grant. Both `uploadId` and `markdownUploadId` have to be completed uploads; otherwise the answer is `409 upload_incomplete` (v1.6.1). An upload the worker found altered answers `409 upload_hash_mismatch` (v1.8).
AAD continuity: the uploaded object is encrypted with AAD `leave:v1:contract:<uploadId>` (or `file`) before the contract exists, and keeps that AAD forever; `GET /contracts/{id}` returns `uploadId` so the app can decrypt the original. Derived `contractField` records use `leave:v1:contractField:<contractId>`.

`GET /contracts` → `[{ contractId, uploadId, status, brand: { name, domain, colors, logoUrl }, payments: [{ dueDate, status }], teammateWrapped: bool, createdAt, contentType }]` (v1.6.1): every contract on the account, newest first by `createdAt` (ISO 8601), without pagination. The fields are plaintext and mean the same as in `GET /contracts/{id}`; `contentType` is the one declared for the original upload. `encryptedFields` and `failure` are only in `GET /contracts/{id}`.
**Changing the contract explanation's schema** (`encryptedFields`, `ContractExtraction`; 2026-10-02). The server validates the explanation against its schema before the phone encrypts it, and can never read or migrate it afterwards, so every build reads every explanation ever written. Adding a field is safe; a reader treats any field added after version 1 as optional forever, since older explanations lack it. A field a build reads is never removed, renamed or retyped. A field no longer filled keeps a neutral value of its own type until the lowest supported build passes every build that reads it as non-null; only then may it become nullable. No change at all, a new `severity` included, until the lowest supported build has a parser for it (the first is build 43). The schema is locked by two samples generated from it in the server's tests and run through each released build's own parser.

`GET /contracts/{id}` → `{ contractId, uploadId, status: "processing"|"pendingOk"|"ready"|"failed", brand: { name, domain, colors, logoUrl }, encryptedFields, payments: [{ dueDate, status: "upcoming"|"paid"|"late" }], teammateWrapped: bool, failure?: { code } }`. `encryptedFields` is one AES-256-GCM blob (base64url `nonce ‖ ciphertext ‖ tag`, AAD `leave:v1:contractField:<contractId>`) of the whole extraction JSON `{ term, deliverables, usageRights, paymentDates, exclusivity, termination, flags[], brand }`, present from `pendingOk` on (the worker writes the fields and sets `pendingOk` together, so the athlete can read the terms before saying they look right) and `null` while `processing` or `failed`; `brand`, `payments`, `status` and `teammateWrapped` are plaintext. `failure.code` appears only with status `failed`: `not_found`, `no_key`, `ocr_not_allowed`, `no_text`, `extraction_failed`, `hash_mismatch` (v1.8: the stored ciphertext is not the one the phone hashed) or `internal` (v1.6). When processing finishes the worker sends `contract_ready` or `contract_failed` (section 11, v1.7, `content-available`), so the app refreshes `GET /contracts/{id}` at once; polling stays as the fallback for a push that is late, switched off or never delivered (about every 5 s while `processing`, then every 15 s).
`POST /contracts/{id}/confirm` → `204` (Pending your OK → saved).
`DELETE /contracts/{id}` → `204`; deletes object, fields, embeddings, graph nodes and all wraps.
`GET /files` → `[{ fileId, uploadId, contentType (as declared), sizeBytes, createdAt }]`; `GET /files/{id}` → `{ fileId, uploadId, url, expiresAt }` with a 15-minute signed read of the object exactly as uploaded (decrypt with the file DEK and AAD `leave:v1:file:<uploadId>`); `DELETE /files/{id}` → `204`.
`GET /storage` → `{ usedBytes, quotaBytes }`.

Client platform notes: the app's deployment target is iOS 17 (CryptoKit HPKE); `POST /talk` is consumed with an XHR-based SSE client because React Native's fetch does not stream, so the server sends `text/event-stream` with heartbeats every 15 s.

Client-side conversion notes (from the app plan, recorded here so the server matches): DOCX is converted on the phone (unzip, read `word/document.xml`); OCR confidence under 0.6 triggers the "send to Google's OCR?" prompt; cloud pickers use `ASWebAuthenticationSession` with PKCE and keep the token in memory only.

## 8. Web, Talk, pitches

`POST /web/run` (grant with strand DEKs) → `{ runId, newConnections, remainingToday, usage, reason? }`. v1.10: the server takes every strand in the grant that has an embedding (the newest 50), decrypts the embeddings in memory, and for each one queries the public HNSW index for the nearest signals, restricted to the athlete's market when the public record has one (`market`) and leaving out expired signals. Candidates are merged (one per signal, keeping the closest strand) and at most 40 go to Gemini 3.8 Flash with the text of the strand each came from; each thread it keeps names that strand (`strandId`). Threads are written encrypted under new DEKs (share-wrap pending, section 6). When no strand in the grant has an embedding, the answer is `200 { runId: null, newConnections: 0, remainingToday, usage, reason: "no_strands" }` and the run is not counted against the 4 a day. When the model declines, the answer is `200` with a `runId`, `newConnections: 0` and `reason: "refused"`, and the run is counted. `reason` is absent otherwise. After 4 counted runs in a UTC day the answer is `429 web_run_cap`.

Thread text (v1.10): the plaintext of a new thread (inside `encrypted`, AAD `leave:v1:thread:<recordId>`) is UTF-8 JSON `{ "v": 1, "headline": string (at most 80 characters), "body": string (at most 1,200), "open": string (at most 200) | null }`. `headline` and `open` state what is open, due or asked, as information, never an instruction; `body` is why it fits. The server checks the lengths and drops a thread that does not fit them. Threads written before v1.10 hold plain text, so a plaintext that does not parse as this JSON is shown as the body.

`GET /web` → `{ strands: [{ recordId, encrypted, createdAt, source: "talk" | "mcp" | "app" }], signals: [...public...], connections: [{ recordId, encrypted, strandId: string | null, createdAt, blockedUntil?, evidence: [{ signalId, title, url, kind, market, brandName | null, publishedAt | null }] }] }`, both lists newest first (v1.10). `strandId` is null for a thread written before v1.10 or whose strand was deleted. Evidence is copied from the public signal when the thread is written; for older threads the fields beyond `signalId`, `title` and `url` are filled from the signal while it still exists, and are null once it is gone.
`POST /talk` (SSE; grant with the strands and contract the turn touches) `{ text?, audioTranscript?, context: { contractId? }, history?: [{ role: "user"|"assistant", text }], summary? }` → events `token`, `receipt` (encrypted change list with undo tokens), `done { usage, summary?, summarizedTurns? }`.

Receipt event (v1.10): `receipt` `{ receiptId, encrypted, changes }`. `encrypted` is the `receipt` record (AAD `leave:v1:receipt:<receiptId>`, key pending, section 6): the JSON list of strand changes `[{ kind, text, recordId }]`, possibly empty. `changes` is plaintext with two kinds of item. A strand item `{ recordId, kind: "strand.add" | "strand.edit", undoToken }` is a strand the server saved (and embedded, section 6). A record item `{ kind: "record.update", field, before, after }` is a change to the public record that the athlete stated in the turn: `field` is one of `displayName`, `sport`, `position`, `team`, `school`, `classYear`, `market`, `height`, `stats`; `before` is the current value (null when there is none, or no record) and `after` the proposed one, checked with the same rules as `PUT /account/public-record` (a proposal that fails them, or equals `before`, is dropped). The server changes nothing in the public record: the app shows the proposal, and it is applied only through `PUT /account/public-record`. A record item carries no `undoToken` and no `recordId`. A turn with only record items still sends a `receipt`; a turn with nothing to save sends none. Record items are proposed on athlete accounts only.

Talk context (v1.8). Talk turns are never stored in plaintext on the server (receipts are encrypted records and the phone holds the transcript), so the phone sends the context with each request and the server keeps nothing new. `history` is the most recent earlier turns, oldest first: at most 20 turns, each 1 to 8,000 characters, 24,000 characters in all; `summary` is the string the server returned in the previous turn's `done`, at most 2,000 characters. Past any of these the answer is `400 invalid_request`. The prompt is the summary (in the system instruction), the newest turns that fit the prompt window (at most 8 turns and 8,000 characters) as native turns, then the saved notes, the contract fields and the new turn; the notes fill what remains of the context cap. When `history` holds turns older than the window, the server folds them into the summary with one short low-thinking model call and returns the result as `done.summary`, with `summarizedTurns`, the number of leading `history` turns it now covers; the phone keeps that summary, sends it with the next turn, and may leave those turns out of `history`. Without older turns, or while the AI kill switch is on (the older turns are then simply left out), `done` has no `summary` and the phone keeps sending the one it has. Every model call of a turn (the reply, receipts, the summary) is metered in the turn's one `usage_events` row, inside the same daily token and monthly spend caps.
`POST /talk/undo` `{ undoToken }` → `204`. v1.10: a token has the form `u1.<strandId>.<issuedAt>.<mac>` and works for 24 hours after the strand was saved; after that the answer is `400 undo_expired`. A token that does not verify, belongs to another account, or has the form used before v1.10 answers `400 undo_invalid`, so tokens issued before v1.10 no longer work.
`POST /pitch` `{ connectionId }` (grant) → `{ pitchId, encryptedDraft, usage }`.

## 9. Usage and billing

The Base plan is $49 a month with a 14-day free trial (14 days since 2026-10-01; a trial already running keeps its length); its monthly allowances (300 talks, 5 contracts / 100 pages, 30 pitch drafts, 120 speaking minutes, 1 GB of files, Web runs up to 4 a day) and the hard caps are listed in section 2b of `architecture-and-security.md`. Extra usage is Leave credits, 1,000 for $10, bought on the web.

`GET /usage` → `{ periodStart, periodEnd, trial: bool, allowances: { talks: { used, included }, contractPages: {...}, contracts: {...}, pitches: {...}, speakingMinutes: {...}, webRunsToday: { used, cap } }, credits: { balance, autoRefill: { enabled, monthlyCapUsd } } }`.
Every metered response includes `usage: { talks: [used, included], credits: balance, nudge: null | "80" | "100" }`.
`GET /billing/portal` → `{ url }` (Stripe Customer Portal; the app opens it in Safari). `POST /billing/subscribe` → `{ url }` (Stripe Checkout for the $49 Base plan with a 14-day free trial (14 days since 2026-10-01; a trial already running keeps its length), card up front, once per account) or `{ portalUrl }` when a subscription already exists; holder only (`guardian_billing` for 13 to 17). v1.11.4: an athlete whose plan a guardian pays gets `409 guardian_plan_active` ("Your plan is paid by your parent or guardian.") unless the body is `{ acknowledgeGuardianPlan: true }`, sent only after the app has shown that fact; credit packs need no flag. `POST /billing/credits/checkout` `{ pack: 1000 }` → `{ url }` (replaces the earlier GET). `PUT /billing/auto-refill` `{ enabled, monthlyCapUsd }` → `204`. `GET /usage` also returns `plan: { status: "none" | "incomplete" | "trialing" | "active" | "past_due" | "canceled" | "unpaid", trialEnd, periodEnd, cancelAtPeriodEnd, paidBy?, covers? }`, from the best of the athlete's own subscription and the guardian's; Stripe's `incomplete_expired` and `paused` are reported as `canceled`. AI routes answer `402 subscription_required` when the status is `none`, `incomplete`, `canceled`, `incomplete_expired` or `unpaid`; `trialing`, `active` and `past_due` have access. Checkout and portal sessions return to fixed static pages on leaveyouragent.com (`/billing/done`, `/billing/cancelled`, with `?credits=1` for credit packs); the app starts every purchase with its own token and opens the URL in Safari, and refetches `GET /usage` on foreground. Credits are a Postgres ledger only; Stripe sells the packs and the plan (v1.4, 2026-09-27).

Billing details as built (v1.7 note, matching the code): `GET /usage` returns `{ periodStart, periodEnd, trial, allowances, credits, plan }`; `allowances` holds `talks`, `contractPages`, `contracts`, `pitches`, `speakingMinutes` as `{ used, included }` and `webRunsToday` as `{ used, cap }`; `credits` is `{ balance, autoRefill: { enabled, monthlyCapUsd } }`; `plan` is always present. The top-level `trial`, the period and `included` follow the plan (v1.7): with a subscription that has access (`trialing`, `active` or `past_due`; the guardian's for an athlete aged 13 to 17), `periodStart` and `periodEnd` are the subscription's current billing period, `trial` is true exactly while `plan.status` is `trialing`, and `included` holds the trial allowances while trialing and the Base allowances otherwise; `used` counts within the same period, so allowances renew on the Stripe billing date. Without such a subscription (`none`, or a plan that ended) the period is the UTC calendar month, `included` shows the trial allowances and `trial` is false; the AI routes answer `402 subscription_required` in that state. `POST /billing/subscribe` reads an optional body, `{ acknowledgeGuardianPlan?: bool }` (v1.11.4; an empty body sent as JSON answers `400 invalid_request`); it answers `{ portalUrl }` for any subscription not in `canceled` or `incomplete_expired`, otherwise `{ url }`, and a second subscribe simply has no trial. `GET /billing/portal` creates the Stripe customer when there is none. `POST /billing/credits/checkout` answers `{ url, credits: 1000 }`; 1,000 is the only pack and no active plan is required to buy it. `PUT /billing/auto-refill` takes `monthlyCapUsd` as an integer from 10 to 1,000 (default 20); one refill is one 1,000-credit pack for $10, triggered when the balance falls below 100 credits and capped per UTC calendar month. Billing error codes: `403 guardian_billing` (athletes aged 13 to 17; it lifts on the 18th birthday, v1.11.4), `409 guardian_plan_active` (v1.11.4), `403 athlete_billing` (a Teammate account), `403 billing_frozen` (purchases paused on the account). The server reads no storefront or country; the US-storefront rule for purchase links is applied in the app.
Who pays (v1.11.4): an athlete's plan is the better of their own subscription and that of the parent or guardian who invited them, while the guardian's account exists. Removing the guardian as Teammate at 18 ends reading, not paying: the guardian's plan keeps covering the athlete until the guardian cancels or deletes their account, or the athlete subscribes, when the athlete's own plan wins at once. `GET /usage` carries, for an athlete, `plan.paidBy: "guardian" | "self" | null` (null when there is no plan) and, for a guardian, `plan.covers: [{ firstName }]`, the athletes their plan currently covers (first names only).

Accounts for athletes aged 13 to 17: billing routes require the parent or guardian's token. Credits never expire while the subscription is active; they are forfeited 30 days after it ends except where a refund is required by law (the `credits` balance is zeroed by the reminders job on day 30 after cancellation).

## 10. MCP clients

Full design in `mcp.md`.

Canonical resource: `https://mcp.leaveyouragent.com` with MCP served at the root path, so the address the athlete types is exactly the protected-resource metadata `resource` (Claude requires the match). MCP spec 2026-07-28, stateless; the server uses SDK v2 (`@modelcontextprotocol/server` + `@modelcontextprotocol/fastify`, `createMcpHandler` in stateless mode), which serves the 2025 spec too. No custom OAuth scope (WorkOS answers unknown scopes with `invalid_scope`, tested live 2026-09-23): clients request `openid offline_access`, the protected-resource metadata omits `scopes_supported`, and `WWW-Authenticate` omits `scope`. Access is decided by `aud = https://mcp.leaveyouragent.com` plus the user, and the server enforces the per-client private flag, so turning private access on never forces a re-authorization.

Connecting happens on the web only (the hosted WorkOS page inside the AI app's sign-in sheet) and grants public access only. There is no consent universal link and no `/mcp/consent` route: listing auth.leaveyouragent.com as an associated domain would let the installed app capture the OAuth page mid-flow inside the system auth sheet ChatGPT and Claude use, breaking sign-in. Private access is turned on afterwards, in the app.

`GET /mcp/clients` → `[{ clientId, name, provider: "chatgpt"|"claude"|"copilot"|"gemini"|"lechat"|"perplexity"|"grok"|"cursor"|"vscode"|"other", private: bool, unlockedUntil?, connectedAt, lastUsedAt }]`. Provider is derived from the host of the CIMD `client_id` URL or from the DCR `redirect_uri` hosts, never from `client_name` alone (v1.9). The `copilot` value exists only for Copilot Studio clients that connect on their own; Leave does not market Copilot.
`PUT /mcp/clients/{id}/scopes` `{ private: bool }` device-signed when turning private on → `204`. The app shows the provider's retention notice before enabling. Accounts aged 13 to 17 cannot share private data with an AI app: the server refuses `private: true` and refuses `POST /mcp/clients/{id}/grant` for these accounts with `error.code = "minor_private_blocked"`, and the app does not show the toggle.
`DELETE /mcp/clients/{id}` → `204` (revokes the WorkOS grant and drops any in-memory keys).
`POST /mcp/clients/{id}/grant` body = a grant (section 1) for that client's records plus `{ ttlSeconds: 900 | 3600 }` (15 minutes default, 1 hour maximum) → `204`. `DELETE /mcp/clients/{id}/grant` → `204` ("Lock now"). MCP grants are held in API process memory only, never in Postgres or a cache; a scale-out or restart means the next private read asks for an unlock again. A private read with no live grant returns a friendly "Open Leave to unlock private data for <client>" tool result and sends the `mcp_unlock_request` push; one tap in the app posts a new grant. Public tools and all writes work at any time.

MCP OAuth tokens (WorkOS AuthKit): access tokens last 5 minutes (the WorkOS production default; Disconnect does not wait for them to lapse, see below), refresh tokens 30 days rotated on use, PKCE S256, resource indicator `https://mcp.leaveyouragent.com` checked by the server as `aud`. Issuer `https://auth.leaveyouragent.com`, JWKS at `/oauth2/jwks`. WorkOS sessions sign out after 30 days idle, so connectors do not sign athletes out every few days. Client ID metadata documents and dynamic client registration are both on (DCR is deprecated in the 2026-07-28 spec but still used by clients). Verified live on 2026-09-23: custom domain issuer, JWKS path, CIMD and DCR both on, S256, token auth `none`, loopback redirects on any port, the resource configured. Still open: `iss` in the authorization response (needs a real sign-in once the MCP server exists). The code input must render with `autocomplete="one-time-code"`.

Tools (model-free except `add_contract`, which enters the contract pipeline): `get_profile` (public record), `get_public_signals`, `list_contracts` (metadata only), `get_contract` (private, needs unlock), `get_web_threads` (public parts always; private strand text only when unlocked), `add_note` (write; becomes an encrypted strand, share-wrap pending), `add_contract` (write; enters the contract pipeline and draws from the contracts allowance), `unlock_private` (sends the push). No delete or send tools. Per-client limits: 60 calls a minute, 600 a day; every call writes a `usage_events` row with feature `mcp`.

Same release as v1.9: Disconnect takes effect on the server at once and deletes the WorkOS authorization; a disconnected client comes back only through a new sign-in, which sends `mcp_connected` again and starts with private sharing off. Lock now, turning private sharing off, Disconnect and Revoke key each end an unlock on every instance (a lock counter on the client row; the keys themselves remain in memory only), and undoing a key revoke does not restore an unlock. `mcp_unlock_request` is sent at most once a minute and five times an hour per client, and not while an unlock is live. `unlock_private` is annotated `readOnlyHint: false`, `destructiveHint: false`, `idempotentHint: true`. The provider is derived from the client's CIMD host or its redirect hosts, never from its name alone. `add_contract` has the app's limits (60 pages, the daily upload cap, the page allowance and credits), with the tool errors `contract_too_long`, `upload_cap`, `allowance_exhausted`, `note_cap` and `empty_note`. The usage event for each MCP call records the tool and an outcome: `ok`, `private`, `locked`, `refused` or `error`. A token without a client id is refused. A request whose `Origin` is outside the allowlist gets `403`. The MCP host is throttled at 1,200 requests a minute per IP, without a ban.

Distribution: Claude users add Leave as a custom connector until Leave can apply for the Claude directory listing, which waits for funding.

## 11. Events and push

`POST /events` `[{ name, ts, props }]` → `202`. Pseudonymous ID: a random UUID generated on install, stored in the Keychain (not synced), sent as `X-Leave-Install`; the server never joins it to the account ID. Names from the design: `tour_started`, `tour_step_viewed`, `tour_skipped`, `tour_completed`, `first_action_picked`, plus `usage_nudge_shown`, `credits_purchased`.

Push payloads (FCM to APNs, `content-available` where noted; never private content):
- `new_sign_in`: `{ type, deviceName, model, city, at }`
- `device_approval_request`: `{ type, approvalRequestId, deviceName, model }`
- `new_public_connection`: `{ type, market, signalKind }` (text like "A new casting call in Philadelphia")
- `usage_nudge`: `{ type, allowance, percent }`
- `web_run_wanted`: `{ type }` (`content-available`, prompts a background matching run)
- `payment_due`: `{ type, contractId, dueDate }` (the brand name is shown only after decryption in the app)
- `mcp_connected`: `{ type, clientId, clientName, provider }` (refreshes Connections and doubles as a security alert; the app names the provider and offers Disconnect)
- `mcp_unlock_request`: `{ type, clientId, clientName, provider }` (one tap opens the unlock sheet; the app posts a grant with the chosen duration, 15 minutes or 1 hour; never sent to accounts aged 13 to 17)
- `billing`: `{ type, reason }` with `reason` one of `trial_will_end`, `payment_failed`, `payment_action_required`, `refill_failed` (never an amount or card detail; the app refetches `GET /usage` and, for the payment reasons, offers the Customer Portal) (v1.5, 2026-09-27)
- `recovery_fetched`: `{ type }` (the recovery blob was fetched; section 5)
- `contract_ready`: `{ type, contractId }` (`content-available`; the contract reached `pendingOk`) (v1.7)
- `contract_failed`: `{ type, contractId, code }` (`content-available`; `code` as `failure.code` in section 7) (v1.7)
- `export_ready`: `{ type, exportId }` (the archive is built; the app reads `GET /export/{id}` for the link) (v1.8)
- `account_update`: `{ type, reason: "adult" }` (v1.11.4): sent once, on or after the 18th birthday, to an athlete who has a parent or guardian link; the app reads `GET /account` for what changed. It says nothing about age or the guardian.
- `deletion_requested`: `{ type }` (v1.11.10): a deletion of the account was requested from a phone that is not approved; sent at the request and again in the 24 hours before it runs, to the account's approved phones and, for an athlete aged 13 to 17, the guardian's. The app reads `GET /account/deletion-request`.
- `deletion_request_cancelled`: `{ type }` (v1.11.10): that request was cancelled, by either phone or by an approved phone using Leave.

Delivery (v1.7): Firebase Cloud Messaging HTTP v1, to every approved, non-removed device of the account that registered with `PUT /devices/{id}/push-token`; a device waiting for approval receives nothing until it is approved. Each push is an FCM data message whose keys are the payload's fields as strings (null fields left out), plus an APNs alert with one fixed sentence per type that carries nothing from the payload: `new_sign_in` "A new sign-in on your Leave account.", `device_approval_request` "A new device is waiting for approval on your Leave account.", `new_public_connection` "A new public connection is in your Web.", `usage_nudge` "There is an update on your Leave allowance.", `web_run_wanted` "New public signals are in for your Web.", `payment_due` "A payment date is coming up.", `mcp_unlock_request` "An AI app is asking to read your private data in Leave.", `mcp_connected` "A new AI app was connected to your Leave account.", `recovery_fetched` "Your Leave recovery data was requested.", `billing` "There is an update on your Leave plan.", `contract_ready` "A contract is ready to read.", `contract_failed` "A contract could not be read.", `export_ready` "Your Leave export is ready to download.", `account_update` "There is an update to your Leave account.", `deletion_requested` "A deletion of your Leave account was requested from another phone.", `deletion_request_cancelled` "A deletion request on your Leave account was cancelled." Registrations FCM reports as unregistered are removed. A push that cannot be sent never fails the request that caused it.

Email (v1.10): product email goes out through Resend from `Leave <chris@leaveyouragent.com>`, with the same address as reply-to, as plain text with a simple HTML part: the Teammate invite and the athlete invite (section 3) and export ready (section 5). There is no welcome email, and sign-in codes come from the sign-in provider. Like a push, an email carries nothing private (first names, and a link to https://leaveyouragent.com), and a send that fails never fails the request or task that caused it.

## 12. Age and consent

Roles: `athlete` (an adult who holds their own account, or an athlete aged 13 to 17 invited by a parent or guardian), `guardian` (the parent or legal guardian who holds the account for an athlete aged 13 to 17 and is the required Teammate), `teammate` (an adult, 18 or older, whose account is opened only from a pending Teammate invite an athlete sent to that email, v1.11; see section 3). **Minimum age is 13** (decided 2026-09-23). Ages are computed on the calendar date at UTC−11 (v1.11.4), the zone of American Samoa, which is never ahead of any US user's own date, so an age line is crossed up to a day late at worst and never early. An athlete with no stored date counts as 13 to 17. The 13 to 17 rules are computed at each request, so they end on the 18th birthday with nothing to run. `POST /auth/verify` for a new account is followed by `POST /account/profile` with the full `birthDate`; the server computes age and rejects under 13 with `error.code = "age_minimum"`, creating no account. For 13 to 17 the parent or guardian creates their own account, gives the third-party disclosure consent (`POST /consent`, device-signed), invites the athlete with `POST /athlete/invite`, is linked as the athlete's required Teammate, and holds billing. The athlete has their own account, share and devices, so private strands are never readable by the guardian. Athletes aged 13 to 17 cannot share private data with AI apps (section 10). A Teammate account (v1.11) is refused under 18 with `teammate_age` and keeps its pending account. There is no under-13 path.

The talent table holds records only for athletes who have signed up; an athlete's public record is looked up at sign-up, and no profile is pre-built for anyone who is not a user.
